\begin{abstract}
Digital systems are at the heart of computer engineering: physical quantities are sampled,
quantized, processed by algorithms and converted back into actions on the physical world. This
paper analyzes this digital chain in a compact thermal chamber controlled by an ESP32. The
temperature measured by an LM35 sensor is sampled by a 12-bit analog-to-digital converter, filtered
by a moving average, processed by a discrete-time proportional-integral (PI) controller and
converted into heating power by a 10-bit pulse-width modulation (PWM) signal. The operating modes
and safety conditions are organized as a finite state machine. The resolution of each stage, the
effect of the digital filter and the discrete implementation of the controller are discussed.
\pendente{Summarize the results: measured noise before and after the filter, and the closed-loop
performance of the discrete PI.}
\end{abstract}

\keywords{Digital systems, Quantization, Digital filter, Discrete-time control, Finite state machine.}
